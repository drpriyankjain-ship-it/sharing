Escalation rules: emergency triggers and safety rules

These are the fixed rules the app applies to every consultation, after the AI and whatever the AI says. They can only make a case more urgent, never less. Please mark each section Agree or Change; it takes about 15 minutes. Your answers stay on this device until you press Send via Gmail: Gmail opens with your answers filled in, and you press Send. Copy answers and Download also work.

How the app uses these rules

  • The AI drafts a plan and a risk level: LOW (the patient can safely wait at the clinic, untreated, for up to 1 hour) or HIGH (needs the doctor first).
  • Then these rules run. If any rule fires, the case becomes HIGH, whatever the AI said.
  • Every plan, LOW or HIGH, waits for the remote doctor's decision before the nurse gives any medicine. The doctor is expected to decide within 1 hour; if not, the nurse phones the doctor.
  • New: a few of these rules now mark a case as an EMERGENCY (section 1). Then the nurse phones the doctor at once instead of waiting.

1 · Emergency: phone the doctor now

When one of these fires, the nurse sees in red at the top of the result: "EMERGENCY — phone the doctor now; do not wait for the dashboard. Danger signs: … Give treatment only as the doctor tells you on the phone, and arrange transfer." The doctor's decision on the phone counts, and the doctor records it in the portal afterwards. The nurse never gives treatment on the AI plan alone.

Not emergencies, though they still make the case HIGH: systolic BP ≥ 180, heart rate > 130 or < 50, temperature > 40.0 °C or < 35.0 °C, glucose > 300 mg/dL, and every diagnosis, medicine and patient rule below. Symptoms the AI records as red flags (for example "vomiting everything", "seizures") are free text, so they never trigger an emergency on their own; they still push the AI's risk level towards HIGH.

For example: should seizures, inability to drink, or a very high blood pressure in pregnancy also mean “phone now”? Those need a new tick-box for the nurse, so say which ones.

3 · Possible sepsis screen

From the ICMR antimicrobial guideline's adult acute-fever protocol: temperature ≥ 37.8 °C (100 °F) with any of RR > 24/min, systolic BP < 90 mmHg or GCS < 15 means possible sepsis: hospitalise. Applied to adults and to patients whose age is unknown; not to a known child under 18, whose normal breathing rate is higher.

6 · Pregnancy and the weeks after delivery

  • A woman counts as pregnant for these rules if she is pregnant or gave birth in the last 6 weeks (42 days). The nurse now records this at registration for any woman aged 12–50 or of unknown age.
  • Pregnancy alone does not make a case HIGH. It does together with a pregnancy-sensitive diagnosis or a medicine to avoid in pregnancy (lists below).
  • If a woman aged 12–50 has an unknown pregnancy status and one of those diagnoses or medicines, the case also becomes HIGH.

Pregnancy-sensitive diagnoses: ectopic pregnancy; pre-eclampsia; eclampsia; anaemia in pregnancy; postpartum sepsis; puerperal sepsis; puerperal fever; maternal sepsis; hyperemesis gravidarum; placenta praevia; abruption; threatened miscarriage; miscarriage; antepartum haemorrhage; gestational diabetes; obstetric cholestasis; malaria; severe malaria; typhoid; tuberculosis; urinary tract infection; epilepsy; hypertension.

7 · Patient and confidence rules

  • Infant under 2 years → HIGH.
  • Weight under 5 kg → HIGH.
  • A medicine the patient reports an allergy to → HIGH.
  • The AI's own confidence in the diagnosis is low → HIGH.
