Pre-referral care for a postpartum woman in septic shock

SBAR summary of a quality review, with one decision for you. It takes about 10 minutes. Your answers stay on this device until you press Send by email (your email app opens with the answers filled in, and you press Send). Copy answers and Download also work.

S Situation

The CDST app helps CHOs (sub-centre) and nurse practitioners (NPs, at the PHC) run a consultation and draft a plan, which a remote doctor reviews. In a quality review we replayed one real consultation through the app many times:

  • The patient: a 28-year-old woman, about 5 weeks (34 days) after delivery. She had fever and body ache for 6–7 days, headache and severe weakness, and was pale.
  • Her vitals: temperature 38.5 °C, BP 81/45 mmHg, pulse 90/min, SpO₂ 100 %. Recorded weight 29.2 kg, height 140 cm (the weight may be a recording error).
  • The app was right on risk and referral: high risk and immediate referral in every run.
  • The pre-referral treatment it wrote was inconsistent. No antibiotic was ever prescribed.

B Background

What the loaded guidelines contain

  • AMRTG 2025 sepsis and puerperal sepsis sections (§3.4, §6.8) are hospital-level. They cover piperacillin-tazobactam or meropenem for 7–14 days, 30 ml/kg crystalloid and vasopressors. We have marked them as background for the referral, never as a prescription source.
  • There is no adult or maternal pre-referral protocol for the sub-centre or PHC. The ICMR workflows cover sepsis only for children and newborns.

A Assessment

  • The sources disagree:
    • Oral vs IV antibiotics in shock: SBA 2010 uses oral antibiotics; CEmONC 2024 forbids them in shock.
    • Fluid volume: no volume (SBA), 20 ml/kg (2021) or 30 ml/kg (2024).
    • Antibiotic choice: ampicillin + gentamicin ± metronidazole, or a cephalosporin.
    None is written for both of our cadres.
  • No source defines when to give it. "General condition poor" is undefined. By the 2009 pulse rule, this woman (pulse 90, BP 81/45) would count as "non-toxic" despite being in shock.
  • Weight-based fluids depend on the recorded weight. At the recorded 29.2 kg, 20 ml/kg is 584 ml and 30 ml/kg is 876 ml; at 50 kg they are 1,000 and 1,500 ml.
  • The app will not invent a regimen. Until you choose one, the safe default stays: high risk, immediate referral, IV access advised, no drug.
